Larnaca offers an extraordinary journey through millennia of human civilization and natural beauty, from a Neolithic settlement dating to 7000 BC to sacred sites revered by both Christian and Islamic traditions. The area's diverse attractions include one of the eastern Mediterranean's best-preserved prehistoric villages, a Byzantine church built over an important biblical tomb, and one of Islam's most significant pilgrimage destinations set beside a striking salt lake. This same wetland complex serves as a vital haven for migratory birds, adding ecological significance to the region's rich historical and spiritual heritage. Together, these sites reveal Larnaca as a crossroads of ancient cultures, religious devotion, and enduring natural landscapes.

# Sites
- [Church of Saint Lazarus, Larnaca](/sites/church-of-saint-lazarus.md) - A late-9th century Greek Orthodox church in Larnaca built over the reputed tomb of Lazarus of Bethany.
- [Hala Sultan Tekke](/sites/hala-sultan-tekke.md) - A mosque and mausoleum complex on Larnaca Salt Lake, considered one of Islam's most important pilgrimage sites.
- [Choirokoitia](/sites/choirokoitia.md) - A UNESCO World Heritage Neolithic settlement from 7000 BC, one of the eastern Mediterranean's best-preserved prehistoric sites.
- [Larnaca Salt Lake](/sites/larnaca-salt-lake.md) - A complex of four interconnected salt lakes west of Larnaca, Cyprus's second-largest salt lake and an important wetland for migratory birds.
