Paphos brings together some of Cyprus's most significant ancient and sacred sites, spanning from Hellenistic and Roman times through the Byzantine era. The area's attractions showcase remarkable archaeological preservation, from rock-carved tombs and monasteries to exceptional mosaics and urban remains of the ancient capital. Visitors can explore UNESCO World Heritage Sites, early Christian hermitages with Byzantine frescoes, and the legendary coastal rock formation where Aphrodite is said to have emerged from the sea. This concentration of historical and mythological landmarks makes Paphos a compelling destination for those interested in Cyprus's layered past, from classical antiquity through medieval monasticism.

# Sites
- [Paphos Archaeological Park](/sites/paphos-archaeological-park.md) - Major archaeological site preserving the ancient capital of Cyprus with exceptional Roman mosaics and Hellenistic urban remains.
- [Tombs of the Kings (Paphos)](/sites/tombs-of-the-kings.md) - Ancient necropolis carved from rock north of Paphos, a UNESCO World Heritage Site dating from the 4th century BC.
- [Petra tou Romiou](/sites/petra-tou-romiou.md) - A sea stack off the coast of Paphos, Cyprus, known in mythology as the birthplace of Aphrodite.
- [Agios Neophytos Monastery](/sites/agios-neophytos-monastery.md) - A 12th-century monastery founded by hermit monk Neophytos, carved into rock near Paphos with Byzantine frescoes and cave hermitage.
