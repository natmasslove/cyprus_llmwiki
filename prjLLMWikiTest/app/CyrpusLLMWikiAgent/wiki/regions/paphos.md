Paphos brings together some of Cyprus's most significant archaeological and cultural treasures, spanning from ancient Greek and Roman civilizations through the Byzantine era. The area is distinguished by its UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including remarkably preserved Roman mosaics and rock-carved tombs that reveal the grandeur of ancient Nea Paphos. Beyond its archaeological wealth, Paphos connects visitors to Greek mythology through coastal landmarks and offers insight into medieval monastic life through cave monasteries adorned with Byzantine art. This concentration of historical layers makes Paphos an essential destination for understanding Cyprus's rich cultural heritage from antiquity to the medieval period.

# Sites
- [Paphos Archaeological Park](/sites/paphos-archaeological-park.md) - Major archaeological site in Cyprus preserving the ancient capital of Nea Paphos with exceptional Roman mosaics and urban remains.
- [Tombs of the Kings (Paphos)](/sites/tombs-of-the-kings.md) - Ancient necropolis carved from rock north of Paphos, a UNESCO World Heritage Site dating from the 4th century BC.
- [Petra tou Romiou](/sites/petra-tou-romiou.md) - A sea stack off the coast of Paphos, Cyprus, known in mythology as the birthplace of Aphrodite.
- [Agios Neophytos Monastery](/sites/agios-neophytos-monastery.md) - A 12th-century monastery founded by hermit monk Neophytos, carved into rock near Paphos with Byzantine frescoes and cave hermitage.
