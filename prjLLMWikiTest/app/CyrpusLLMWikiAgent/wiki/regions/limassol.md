Limassol serves as the gateway to some of Cyprus's most significant historical landmarks, spanning over two millennia of Mediterranean civilization. The region showcases the island's layered past through ancient Greek and Roman ruins perched on dramatic coastal cliffs, sacred sanctuaries dedicated to classical deities, and medieval fortifications built by Crusaders and Ottomans. From the extensive archaeological remains of Kourion to the imposing stone walls of Crusader and Ottoman castles, these sites reveal Cyprus's strategic importance as a crossroads of empires and cultures. Whether exploring temples where Apollo was worshipped for over a thousand years or walking through castle halls that once controlled the island's lucrative sugar and wine trade, visitors encounter tangible connections to the ancient, classical, and medieval worlds.

# Sites
- [Kourion](/sites/kourion.md) - Ancient Greek city-state on Cyprus's southwestern coast, with extensive Roman ruins atop a dramatic coastal promontory.
- [Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates](/sites/apollo-hylates.md) - Ancient sanctuary where Apollo Hylates, god of the woodlands, was worshipped from the eighth century BC to the fourth century AD.
- [Kolossi Castle](/sites/kolossi-castle.md) - A 15th-century Crusader castle near Limassol, once a strategic stronghold and center of sugar production and Commandaria wine.
- [Limassol Castle](/sites/limassol-castle.md) - A medieval castle in Limassol's old harbour, rebuilt by the Ottomans in 1590 over earlier Christian and Byzantine structures.
