The Limassol region offers an exceptional journey through Cyprus's layered past, from ancient Greek and Roman civilizations to medieval Crusader rule and Ottoman occupation. Visitors can explore the dramatic clifftop ruins of Kourion with its theatre and the nearby Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates, where the woodland god was venerated for over a millennium. The area's medieval heritage comes alive at two impressive castles: the Crusader-era Kolossi Castle, once central to the island's sugar and wine trade, and Limassol Castle in the old harbour, a structure that bears the architectural marks of Byzantine, Christian, and Ottoman builders. Together, these sites reveal how successive empires and cultures shaped this strategic corner of the Mediterranean over more than two thousand years.

# Sites
- [Kourion](/sites/kourion.md) - Ancient Greek city-state on Cyprus's southwestern coast, with extensive Roman ruins including a theatre and Apollo sanctuary.
- [Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates](/sites/apollo-hylates.md) - Ancient sanctuary where Apollo Hylates, god of the woodlands, was worshipped from the eighth century BC to the fourth century AD.
- [Kolossi Castle](/sites/kolossi-castle.md) - A 15th-century Crusader castle near Limassol, once a strategic stronghold and center of sugar production and Commandaria wine.
- [Limassol Castle](/sites/limassol-castle.md) - A medieval castle in Limassol's old harbour, rebuilt by the Ottomans in 1590 over earlier Christian and Byzantine structures.
