Cyprus's coastline offers a remarkable blend of natural beauty and ecological significance, where dramatic geological formations meet vital wildlife habitats. From the legendary sea stack at Petra tou Romiou rising from the waters off Paphos to the interconnected salt lakes west of Larnaca that serve as crucial stopover points for migratory birds, these sites showcase the island's diverse coastal landscapes. Cape Greco's dramatic headland in the southeast completes this natural trio with its striking sea caves and position along important bird migration routes, demonstrating how Cyprus's shores combine scenic splendor with environmental importance.

# Sites
- [Petra tou Romiou](/sites/petra-tou-romiou.md) - A sea stack off the coast of Paphos, Cyprus, known in mythology as the birthplace of Aphrodite.
- [Larnaca Salt Lake](/sites/larnaca-salt-lake.md) - A complex of four interconnected salt lakes west of Larnaca, Cyprus's second-largest salt lake and an important wetland for migratory birds.
- [Cape Greco](/sites/cape-greco.md) - A dramatic headland in southeastern Cyprus, known for its sea caves, bird migrations, and natural coastal beauty.
