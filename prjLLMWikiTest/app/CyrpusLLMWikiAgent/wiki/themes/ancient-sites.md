Cyprus preserves an extraordinary timeline of human civilization, from prehistoric Neolithic settlements dating to 7000 BC through Greek, Roman, Byzantine, and Crusader periods. The island's ancient and historic sites showcase remarkable archaeological treasures including intricate Roman mosaics, rock-cut tombs, classical theatres, and religious sanctuaries dedicated to ancient gods. Medieval fortifications built by Crusaders and Ottomans stand alongside ruins of Greek city-states and early Christian structures, many recognized as UNESCO World Heritage Sites. These locations span the island from the southwestern coast to inland areas, offering visitors a comprehensive journey through Cyprus's role as a crossroads of Mediterranean cultures and empires.

# Sites
- [Paphos Archaeological Park](/sites/paphos-archaeological-park.md) - Major archaeological site preserving the ancient capital of Cyprus with exceptional Roman mosaics and Hellenistic urban remains.
- [Tombs of the Kings (Paphos)](/sites/tombs-of-the-kings.md) - Ancient necropolis carved from rock north of Paphos, a UNESCO World Heritage Site dating from the 4th century BC.
- [Kourion](/sites/kourion.md) - Ancient Greek city-state on Cyprus's southwestern coast, with extensive Roman ruins including a theatre and Apollo sanctuary.
- [Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates](/sites/apollo-hylates.md) - Ancient sanctuary where Apollo Hylates, god of the woodlands, was worshipped from the eighth century BC to the fourth century AD.
- [Kolossi Castle](/sites/kolossi-castle.md) - A 15th-century Crusader castle near Limassol, once a strategic stronghold and center of sugar production and Commandaria wine.
- [Limassol Castle](/sites/limassol-castle.md) - A medieval castle in Limassol's old harbour, rebuilt by the Ottomans in 1590 over earlier Christian and Byzantine structures.
- [Choirokoitia](/sites/choirokoitia.md) - A UNESCO World Heritage Neolithic settlement from 7000 BC, one of the eastern Mediterranean's best-preserved prehistoric sites.
