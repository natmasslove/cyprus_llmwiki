Cyprus's rich spiritual heritage is embodied in a remarkable collection of monasteries, churches, and sacred sites that span multiple faiths and centuries. From the rock-carved hermitage of Agios Neophytos and the wealth of Kykkos Monastery to the Byzantine frescoes adorning ten UNESCO-listed churches in the Troödos Mountains, these sites showcase the island's deep Christian traditions dating back to the 9th through 12th centuries. The collection also reflects Cyprus's position as a crossroads of civilizations, with Hala Sultan Tekke standing as one of Islam's most significant pilgrimage destinations and the Church of Saint Lazarus marking the legendary connection to biblical history. Whether exploring cave monasteries near Paphos, venerating ancient icons, or visiting sacred tombs in Larnaca, visitors encounter centuries of devotion expressed through exceptional architecture and art.

# Sites
- [Agios Neophytos Monastery](/sites/agios-neophytos-monastery.md) - A 12th-century monastery founded by hermit monk Neophytos, carved into rock near Paphos with Byzantine frescoes and cave hermitage.
- [Church of Saint Lazarus, Larnaca](/sites/church-of-saint-lazarus.md) - A late-9th century Greek Orthodox church in Larnaca built over the reputed tomb of Lazarus of Bethany.
- [Hala Sultan Tekke](/sites/hala-sultan-tekke.md) - A mosque and mausoleum complex on Larnaca Salt Lake, considered one of Islam's most important pilgrimage sites.
- [Kykkos Monastery](/sites/kykkos-monastery.md) - One of Cyprus's wealthiest monasteries, founded in the 11th century, housing an icon attributed to the Apostle Luke.
- [Painted Churches in the Troodos Region](/sites/painted-churches-troodos.md) - UNESCO World Heritage Site comprising ten Byzantine churches with murals in the Troödos Mountains of central Cyprus.
