# Overview
The Painted Churches in the Troödos Region is a UNESCO World Heritage Site located in the Troödos Mountains of central Cyprus. The complex comprises ten Byzantine churches and monasteries richly decorated with Byzantine and post-Byzantine murals. These sites demonstrate the range of architectural and artistic influences affecting Cyprus over the entire Byzantine reign, while displaying architectural elements unique to Cyprus, including steep-pitched wooden roofs with tiling. Nine churches were designated by UNESCO in 1985, with a tenth added in 2001.

# History
In 965 AD, Cyprus was annexed by the Byzantine Empire during the Arab-Byzantine wars. Over a period of 500 years until the 16th century, many Byzantine churches were constructed in the Troödos Region. This region holds one of the greatest concentrations of monasteries and churches in the former Byzantine Empire. The churches range in date from the 11th century to the 16th century, representing the full span of Byzantine rule in Cyprus.

# What to see
The ten churches include the 11th-century Church of Agios Nikolaos tis Stegis in Kakopetria, the oldest surviving katholikon in Cyprus. Notable sites include the 12th-century Church of Panagia Phorviotissa (Asinou) in Nikitari and the 13th-century Agios Ioannis Lampadistis Monastery in Kalopanagiotis. The Church of Panagia in Moutoullas is the earliest example of steep-pitched wooden roofs. The Church of Timios Stavros in Pelendri contains unique 14th-century wall paintings.

# Location & access
The churches are located throughout the Troödos Mountains in central Cyprus. The sites are distributed across various mountain villages including Kakopetria, Kalopanagiotis, Nikitari, Lagoudhera, Moutoullas, Pedoulas, Pelendri, Galata, Platanistasa, and Palaichori Oreinis. The Troödos Region is characterized by mountainous terrain in the central part of the island.
