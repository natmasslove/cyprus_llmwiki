# Overview

Limassol Castle is situated near the old harbour in the heart of the historical centre of Limassol. The castle as it appears today is a structure rebuilt around 1590 under Ottoman rule. Archaeological investigation revealed it was built over an Early Christian basilica from the 4th-7th centuries and a Middle Byzantine monument from the 10th-11th centuries. Finds beneath the castle show the existence of an important church, possibly the city's first cathedral.

# History

According to Etienne Lusignan, the original castle was erected by Guy of Lusignan in 1193, with the first official reference dating to 1228 during Holy Roman Emperor Frederick II's involvement in Cyprus. From its erection until the early 16th century, the castle suffered damages from Genoese and Mamluk attacks and earthquakes, alternating with restorations. After the Ottomans captured Limassol in 1538, the Venetian governor demolished the castle in 1567-1568 to prevent its seizure. Following Ottoman acquisition of Cyprus in 1576, the remains were incorporated into a new Ottoman fort completed in 1590. According to tradition, Richard the Lionheart married Berengaria of Navarre here and crowned her Queen of England in 1191.

# What to see

The castle structure dates primarily from the Ottoman reconstruction of 1590. The underground chamber and first floor were transformed into prison cells and remained in use until 1950. Archaeological remains reveal layers of earlier structures including an Early Christian basilica and Middle Byzantine monument beneath the current building.

# Location & access

The castle is located near the old harbour in the heart of the historical centre of Limassol city. It stands in the urban core of Limassol, Cyprus's second-largest city on the southern coast.
