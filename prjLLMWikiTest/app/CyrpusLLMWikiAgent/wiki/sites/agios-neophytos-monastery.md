# Overview

Saint Neophytos Monastery is one of the best-known monasteries in Cyprus, founded by monk Neophytos in the 12th century. The property consists of the Engleistra, a place of seclusion built in a natural cave with a small chapel, and the main monastery buildings. It currently functions as a museum showcasing the history of the monastic community, icons, and manuscripts. The site features Byzantine wall paintings and architecture spanning several centuries.

# History

Neophytos fled into the hills of Cyprus after being jailed for pursuing an ascetic life, finding a small natural cave in 1159 which he excavated to create his hermitage cell, chapel, and tomb. The hermitage became a small monastic community in 1170 when the Bishop of Paphos convinced him to take a pupil, though Neophytos kept the population to around fifteen or eighteen monks due to his opposition to materialism. After his death, he was buried within the Engleistra according to his own instructions, and his hallows were found in 1756 and placed in the main church. In 1631, Patriarch Cyril Loukaris declared the monastery to have precedence over all other monasteries on Cyprus. The monastery has been continuously inhabited and expanded over the centuries, with major renovations undertaken in 1503 by another monk named Neophytos.

# What to see

The original Engleistra consists of three parts carved into the rock face: the church of the True Cross, the cell of Neophytos, and the Refectory. The main church (katholikon) is a large Venetian-inspired basilica built in the early 16th century. Wall paintings throughout the monastery include works by Theodore Apsuedes from 1183, featuring traditional Byzantine scenes like the Crucifixion and Annunciation, as well as unusual depictions of Neophytos himself alongside archangels. The museum houses icons and manuscripts documenting the monastery's history, and studies have revealed at least five phases of painting using expensive pigments including lapis lazuli, gold, and silver.

# Location & access

The monastery lies in the territory of the community of Tala, 1 km north of the village and 15 km north of Paphos. It is located 4 km west of Tremithousa, across a steep valley from the Tsiárta mountain. The Engleistra is carved into a steep rock face in the hills of Cyprus.
