# Overview

Kourion was an important ancient Greek city-state on the southwestern coast of Cyprus, founded by Greek settlers from Argos in the twelfth century BCE after the collapse of the Mycenaean palaces. The acropolis sits atop a limestone promontory nearly 100 metres high along the coast of Episkopi Bay. The site flourished through Greek, Roman and Byzantine periods before being destroyed by Arab raids in 648–649 CE. The archaeological area is managed by the Cyprus Department of Antiquities and lies within the British Overseas Territory of Akrotiri and Dhekelia.

# History

The Kingdom of Kourion was established during the Cypro-Geometric period (1050–750 BCE) and became one of Cyprus's most influential kingdoms. The city came under successive Assyrian, Egyptian, Persian, Ptolemaic and Roman rule, with King Pasikrates aiding Alexander the Great at the siege of Tyre in 332 BCE. Under Roman rule from 58 BCE, Kourion flourished as a prominent city with civic government, thriving elite and active participation in the imperial cult. Between 365 and 370 CE, five strong earthquakes caused near-complete destruction, though the city was reconstructed in the late fourth and early fifth centuries before Arab raids ended occupation of the acropolis in 648–649.

# What to see

The majority of archaeological remains date to the Roman and Late Roman/Early Byzantine periods. The Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates, located 1.7 km west of the acropolis, was a Pan-Cyprian sanctuary third in importance on the island, with structures dating from the late eighth century BCE through Roman restorations under Trajan. The acropolis features an Early Christian Basilica and various Roman-period structures including the House of Gladiators. Systematic excavations have been conducted since 1934 by institutions including the University of Pennsylvania, the British Museum, and most recently the Kourion Urban Space Project since 2012.

# Location & access

The acropolis of Kourion is located 1.3 km southwest of Episkopi and 13 km west of Limassol on Cyprus's southwestern coast. The site occupies a limestone promontory nearly 100 metres high along Episkopi Bay. The Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates lies 1.7 km west of the acropolis and 0.65 km inland from the coastline. The archaeological area lies within the British Overseas Territory of Akrotiri and Dhekelia.
