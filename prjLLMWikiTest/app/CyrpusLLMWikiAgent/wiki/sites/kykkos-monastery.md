# Overview

Kykkos Monastery (Ιερά Μονή Κύκκου) is one of the wealthiest monasteries in Cyprus, founded around the end of the 11th century by Byzantine emperor Alexios I Komnenos. The monastery houses a revered icon of the Virgin Mary, believed to have been painted by the Apostle Luke. The first president of Cyprus, Archbishop Makarios III, began his ecclesiastical career here as a monk in 1926. No remains of the original monastery survive, as it has been destroyed by fire multiple times throughout its history.

# History

According to tradition, the monastery was founded after a hermit named Esaias requested that the Byzantine governor Manuel Boutoumites bring an icon of the Virgin from Constantinople to Cyprus. When the emperor's daughter fell ill with the same affliction that had struck Boutoumites, the governor convinced Emperor Alexios to send the icon to Cyprus in exchange for her healing. The emperor initially attempted to send a copy, but the Virgin Mary appeared to him in a dream instructing that the original icon be sent to Cyprus. The current church dates to 1745, having been destroyed and rebuilt three times in the monastery's history.

# What to see

The monastery's most significant treasure is the icon of the Virgin Mary, which remains covered as tradition holds that anyone who looks upon it will be blinded. The last person recorded to have seen the icon was the Pope and Patriarch of Alexandria Gerasimos in 1669. To the right of the icon is a bronze arm, commemorating a story of a Turk who was cursed after attempting to light a cigarette from a vigil lamp. The grave of Archbishop Makarios III is located at Throni, 3 km west of the monastery.

# Location & access

Kykkos Monastery lies 20 km west of Pedoulas on the northwest face of the Troodos Mountains at an altitude of 1,318 meters. The monastery maintains separate grounds called Metochion Kykkou in Engomi, Nicosia, acquired through bequests over the years. Archbishop Makarios III was buried at the Metochion grounds after the building was hit by tank fire in 1974.
