# Overview

Cape Greco, also known as Capo Greco, is a headland in the southeastern part of Cyprus at the southern end of Famagusta Bay. It forms part of Ayia Napa Municipality and is visited by tourists for its natural environment. The cape is the easternmost point of both the government-controlled areas of the Republic of Cyprus and, de facto, of the European Union when excluding the outermost regions. According to local legend, it is the home of the 'Ayia Napa sea monster'.

# History

In antiquity, the cape was known as Cape Pedalion. According to Strabo, the trapezoidal hill lying above it was sacred to Aphrodite. A powerful mediumwave broadcasting station operated by Radio Monte Carlo was constructed at Cape Greco in the early 1970s and began operation in 1973, transmitting French- and Arabic-language programmes to the Middle East and Northern Africa. The broadcasting antennas were demolished in early November 2021, with plans for a nature reserve information centre to take their place.

# What to see

The spectacular Sea Caves, sometimes called "Palaces" or "fairy caves", are carved naturally into the striking limestone cliffs along the coast of Cape Greco National Forest Park. The area has been designated an Important Bird Area by BirdLife International because it is a key migration site for large numbers of raptors and other birds. It forms a migratory bottleneck for red-footed falcons, pallid harriers, honey buzzards and common kestrels, and supports breeding populations of Cyprus wheatears, Cyprus warblers and black francolins.

# Location & access

Cape Greco lies between the towns of Ayia Napa and Protaras, both tourist resorts in southeastern Cyprus. The cape is part of a National Forest Park under the administration of the Forestry Department of the Cyprus Ministry of the Interior. It sits at the southern end of Famagusta Bay on the island's southeastern coast.
