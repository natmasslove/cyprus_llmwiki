# Overview

Petra tou Romiou, also known as Aphrodite's Rock, is a sea stack located off the shore of Paphos, Cyprus. The site combines natural beauty with mythological significance as the legendary birthplace of the goddess Aphrodite. The combination of scenic appeal and mythological status makes it a popular tourist destination. The sea in this region is generally rough, and climbing the rock is not permitted.

# History

According to Greek mythology, Aphrodite was born from the sea foam created when Cronus threw the severed genitals of his father Uranus into the ocean at this location. The local version of the legend indicates that Aphrodite's Rock is part of Uranus' lower body, and that the goddess arose from white foam before being carried by waves to Cyprus. Aphrodite attracted a large cult following in Paphos, evident from the Sanctuary of Aphrodite in Old Paphos, Kouklia, which was eventually crushed by the Romans. The present name Petra tou Romiou associates the place with the Byzantine-Arabic hero Basil from the Digenes Akritas, who according to legend hurled a huge rock from the Troodos Mountains to repel invading Saracens.

# What to see

The main feature is the sea stack itself, rising from the foaming waters that gave rise to the Aphrodite birth legend. The rock fragments and rough seas create a dramatic coastal landscape. A nearby rock is known as the Saracen Rock, connected to the legend of Basil. A restaurant, tourist pavilion, and the Aphrodite Hills resort are located in the vicinity.

# Location & access

Petra tou Romiou is located off the shore along the main road from Paphos to Limassol in the Paphos region of Cyprus. The site is positioned on the southwestern coast of the island. The nearby beach is associated with the name Achni, attributed to the Achaeans coming ashore on their return from Troy.
