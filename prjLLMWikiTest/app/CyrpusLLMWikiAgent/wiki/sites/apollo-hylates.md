# Overview

The Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates at Kourion was one of the main religious centres of ancient Cyprus. Apollo Hylates was a god worshipped on Cyprus who was later likened to the Greek god Apollo, revered as god of the woodlands. His name probably derives from the Greek words for "barking" or "forest". The sanctuary was also known from other Cypriot cities including Amamassos, Erystheia, Hyle, and Tembros, with the epithet Hylates possibly originating from Hyle, a location near Kourion.

# History

Worship of Apollo at this site began as early as the eighth century BC and continued until the fourth century AD. The site underwent many extensions and alterations across different periods. Originally it consisted of a temple, a circular monument probably used for processions or dances around sacred trees, and a formalized archaic altar and precinct. During the Roman period the sanctuary was extended with South and North Buildings, and in the first century AD the temple was rebuilt with a different architectural style featuring Nabatean columns.

# What to see

The majority of visible monuments belong to the first century AD restorations. A wall surrounds the sanctuary with the Kourion Gate and Pafos Gate providing entry points. The Temple of Apollo Hylates, built in the Late Classical or Early Hellenistic period on archaic temple ruins, stands at the end of a long street running south to north. Additional structures include a votive pit containing terracotta figurines and pottery from the fifth century BC to the Roman period, a small building that may have been a priest's house, and along the external east walls are the palaestra where athletes exercised and the baths.

# Location & access

The sanctuary is located about 2.5 kilometres west of the ancient town of Kourion. It sits along the road which leads to Pafos on the island of Cyprus.
