# Overview

The Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates at Kourion was one of the main religious centres of ancient Cyprus. Hylates was a god worshipped on Cyprus who was later likened to the Greek god Apollo, with his name probably deriving from words meaning "barking" or "forest". At this sanctuary, Apollo was venerated as god of the woodlands. Worship at the site began as early as the eighth century BC and continued until the fourth century AD.

# History

The sanctuary underwent many extensions and alterations across different periods of its long history. Originally the site consisted of a temple, a circular monument probably used for processions or dances around sacred trees, and a formalized archaic altar and precinct. During the Roman period the site was expanded with South and North Buildings that may have housed votives or accommodated visitors. The temple was rebuilt in the first century AD with a different architectural style featuring Nabatean columns, and the majority of visible monuments today belong to this first century AD restoration.

# What to see

A wall with the Kourion Gate and Pafos Gate surrounds the sanctuary. The Temple of Apollo Hylates, built in the Late Classical or Early Hellenistic period on archaic temple ruins, stands at the end of a long street running south to north. A votive pit contains terracotta figurines and pottery accumulated from the fifth century BC to the Roman period. Along the external east side of the walls are the palaestra where athletes exercised and played games, and the baths. A small building south of the precinct may have been a priest's house.

# Location & access

The sanctuary is located about 2.5 kilometres west of the ancient town of Kourion. It sits along the road that leads to Pafos. According to ancient sources, Apollo Hylates was also worshipped in the Cypriot cities of Amamassos, Erystheia, Hyle, and Tembros, with the epithet Hylates possibly deriving from Hyle, a location near the Kourion region.
