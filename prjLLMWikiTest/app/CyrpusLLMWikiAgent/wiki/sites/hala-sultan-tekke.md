# Overview

Hala Sultan Tekke is a mosque and takya located on the west bank of Larnaca Salt Lake in Cyprus. The complex comprises a mosque, mausoleum, cemetery, and living quarters, built around the tomb of Umm Haram, wife of a companion of Muhammad and foster sister of his mother. The site is regarded as the third or fourth holiest place in Islam by various sources. It is also a listed Ancient Monument due to the significant Bronze Age archaeological remains in the area.

# History

During the Late Bronze Age (around 1650-1100 BC), the area served as a cemetery for a major urban center, one of the largest cities of that period, possibly covering 50 hectares. Excavations beginning in the 1890s and continuing to the present have uncovered rich tombs and evidence of extensive Bronze Age trade networks. According to tradition, Umm Haram died here during the first Arab conquest of Cyprus between 647 and 649 AD. The Ottoman mosque complex was built in stages from 1760 onward, with the shrine constructed by Sheikh Hasan and later additions by various Ottoman governors through the early 19th century.

# What to see

The domed mosque is square-shaped, built in yellow stone blocks with a minaret repaired in 1959. Umm Haram's tomb lies behind the mosque wall facing Mecca, alongside four other tombs including those of former sheikhs and Adile Hüseyin Ali, Turkish wife of Hussein bin Ali, Sharif of Mecca. An octagonal fountain built around 1796-1797 stands opposite the mosque. The entry gate features an Ottoman inscription dated 1813 with Sultan Mahmud II's monogram, and the complex includes a cemetery closed to burials around 1899.

# Location & access

The complex is situated on the west shore of Larnaca Salt Lake in Larnaca, Cyprus. The site lies near significant Bronze Age archaeological remains, with the ancient settlement located a few hundred meters to the west. The location occupies an area that has been important since prehistory, positioned on the shores of the salt lake.
