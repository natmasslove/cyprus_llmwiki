# Overview

Larnaca Salt Lake is a complex network of four salt lakes located to the west of Larnaca city in Cyprus. The largest is Lake Aliki, followed by Lake Orphani, Lake Soros, and Lake Spiro, with a total surface area of 2.2 km². It is the second largest salt lake in Cyprus after the Limassol Salt Lake. The site has been designated a Ramsar wetland, Natura 2000 site, Special Protected Area under the Barcelona Convention, and an Important Bird Area, making it one of Cyprus's most important wetlands.

# History

Salt harvested from the lake was once one of Cyprus's major exports, collected with donkeys and piled into huge pyramidal heaps at the lake's edge. Rising labour costs caused harvesting to dwindle, and it stopped altogether in 1986 as the island began importing salt instead. According to legend, the lake's saltiness originated when St Lazarus cursed an old woman's vines to become a salt lake forever after she refused him food and drink. The lake was declared a protected area by the Council of Ministers in 1997.

# What to see

The lake attracts 85 species of water-birds with populations estimated between 20,000 and 38,000, making it an important migratory passage through Cyprus. Between 2,000 and 12,000 greater flamingoes spend winter months there, feeding on brine shrimp, and recent evidence suggests they also breed at the site. Other notable species include cranes, plovers, gulls, stilts, stone-curlews, and endemic Cypriot warblers. On the lake's bank stands Hala Sultan Tekke, one of the holiest shrines in Ottoman Islam, housing the tomb of Umm Haram, Muhammad's wet-nurse.

# Location & access

The salt lake complex lies to the west of Larnaca city and is situated just off the road leading to Larnaca International Airport, making it one of the area's most distinctive landmarks. The lakes are surrounded by halophytic scrubland adapted to the saline environment. During winter the lake fills with water, while in summer the water evaporates, leaving a crust of salt and grey dust. The salt water penetrates through porous rock between the lake and the sea, creating the highly saline conditions.
