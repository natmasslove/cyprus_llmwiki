# Overview

The Church of Saint Lazarus is a late-9th century church in Larnaca, Cyprus, belonging to the autocephalous Greek Orthodox Church of Cyprus. It is named for Lazarus of Bethany, whom Jesus raised from the dead according to the Gospel of John. According to Eastern Orthodox tradition, Lazarus fled to Cyprus after the Resurrection and was appointed first Bishop of Kition by Paul the Apostle and Barnabas. The church was built over the reputed second tomb where Lazarus was buried after living thirty more years in Cyprus.

# History

In 890, a tomb bearing the inscription "Lazarus, four days dead, friend of Christ" was discovered in Larnaca after being lost during Arab rule beginning in 649. Emperor Leo VI had Lazarus's remains transferred to Constantinople in 898 and built the church in recompense in the late 9th to early 10th centuries. Under Frankish and Venetian rule (13th to 16th centuries), the church became Roman Catholic and a Gothic portico was added. The Ottomans destroyed its three domes and bell tower in 1571 and converted it to a mosque, but sold it back to the Orthodox in 1589, after which it served both Orthodox and Catholic congregations for two centuries. The bell tower was rebuilt in 1857 in Latinate style after Ottoman authorities again permitted bell towers on Cypriot churches.

# What to see

The church is an elongated building measuring 31.5 by 14.5 meters with a tripartite sanctuary and three aisles divided by bulky double pillars supporting domes. The unique Baroque iconostasis was carved between 1773 and 1782 by Chatzisavvas Taliadorou and gold-plated between 1793 and 1797, with icons painted by Michael Proskynetes and Hatzimichael. The church features stonework of square limestone blocks about one meter thick, a Gothic stone portico on the south side, and 17th-century wood-carved furniture including a Rococo pulpit. Human remains identified as part of Saint Lazarus's relics were discovered in a marble sarcophagus under the altar during 1972 renovations. On Lazarus Saturday, eight days before Easter, the icon of Saint Lazarus is carried in procession through Larnaca's streets.

# Location & access

The Church of Saint Lazarus is located in Larnaca, a coastal city on the southern coast of Cyprus. Larnaca was known in ancient times as Kition, where Lazarus served as the first bishop. The church stands in the city center with an open porch from which steps descend into the interior.
