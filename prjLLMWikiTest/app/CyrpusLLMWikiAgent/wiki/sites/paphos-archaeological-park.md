# Overview

Paphos Archaeological Park encompasses the major part of ancient Nea Paphos, which served as the island's capital under Ptolemaic and Roman administrations. The site preserves well-preserved urban infrastructure, public buildings, residential compounds, rock-cut tombs, and a harbor complex. Several villas contain expansive polychrome floor mosaics featuring geometric designs and mythological scenes, considered among the finest preserved in the Mediterranean world. The park forms part of the Paphos UNESCO World Heritage Site and is also known as the Kato Pafos Archaeological Park.

# History

Nea Paphos was likely founded in the late 4th century BC by Nikokles, the last ruler of the Cypriot city-kingdom of Paphos, and flourished as a strategic Ptolemaic shipbuilding center. In the 2nd century BC it became the administrative capital of Cyprus, replacing Salamis, and remained the capital through the Hellenistic and Roman eras. A series of destructive earthquakes in the 4th century AD resulted in the capital moving back to Salamis, though Nea Paphos was rebuilt as an Early Christian bishopric and survived into the Byzantine era. Archaeological excavations began in June 1965 under Polish institutions and continue alongside Cypriot experts and international partners.

# What to see

The site features four elaborate Roman villas named the House of Aion, House of Dionysos, House of Orpheus, and Villa of Theseus, all containing exceptional mosaic floors depicting mythological figures. The agora, one of the largest Hellenistic agoras in the eastern Mediterranean, spans roughly 150 by 160 meters and includes an odeon, asclepeion, and workshops. A Hellenistic-Roman theatre dating to the end of the 4th century BCE occupies the northeastern sector near Fabrika Hill. The 2nd-century AD Odeon, a semi-circular limestone theater with 12 rows of seating, has been restored and hosts cultural events.

# Location & access

The archaeological park is located in Paphos on the southwestern coast of Cyprus. The ancient city occupied a strategic maritime location in the Eastern Mediterranean with its harbor complex. The site sits beneath the acropolis on Phanari Hill near the port, with the theatre positioned on the southern slope of Fabrika Hill overlooking the urban core.
