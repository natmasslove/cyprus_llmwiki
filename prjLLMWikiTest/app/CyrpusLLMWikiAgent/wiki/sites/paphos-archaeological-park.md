# Overview

Paphos Archaeological Park encompasses the major part of ancient Nea Paphos, which served as Cyprus's capital under Ptolemaic and Roman administrations. The site preserves well-preserved urban infrastructure, public buildings, residential compounds, rock-cut tombs, and a harbor complex from a major maritime trading hub in the eastern Mediterranean. Several Roman villas contain expansive polychrome floor mosaics featuring geometric designs and mythological scenes, considered among the finest preserved in the Mediterranean world. The site forms part of the Paphos UNESCO World Heritage Site and is also known as Kato Pafos Archaeological Park.

# History

Nea Paphos was likely founded in the late 4th century BC by Nikokles, the last ruler of the Cypriot city-kingdom of Paphos, and flourished as a strategic Ptolemaic shipbuilding center. In the 2nd century BC it became the administrative capital of Cyprus, replacing Salamis, and remained the capital through the Hellenistic and Roman eras. In the 4th century AD, destructive earthquakes caused the capital to move back to Salamis, though Nea Paphos was rebuilt as an Early Christian bishopric and survived into the Byzantine era. Archaeological excavations began in June 1965 under Polish institutions and continue alongside Cypriot experts and international partners.

# What to see

The four major Roman villas—the House of Dionysos, House of Aion, House of Orpheus, and Villa of Theseus—feature exceptional floor mosaics depicting mythological scenes including Theseus battling the Minotaur, the deity Aion, and the Triumph of Dionysos. The Hellenistic agora, one of the largest in the eastern Mediterranean at 2.5 hectares, served as the city's administrative and economic hub and contained an odeon, asclepeion, and workshops. The theatre dates to the end of the 4th century BCE and occupies the northeastern sector on Fabrika Hill's southern slope. Additional structures include the 2nd-century AD Odeon with 12 rows of seating, the early 5th-century Basilica of Panagia Limeniotissa, and the necropolis known as the Tombs of the Kings.

# Location & access

The archaeological park is located in Kato Paphos on the southwestern coast of Cyprus. The ancient city was built near the harbor with the agora positioned beneath the acropolis on Phanari Hill. The theatre occupies the northeastern sector on the southern slope of Fabrika Hill, offering views across the urban core toward the harbor.
