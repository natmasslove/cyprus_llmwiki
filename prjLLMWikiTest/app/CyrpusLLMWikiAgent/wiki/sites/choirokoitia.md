# Overview

Khirokitia is an archaeological site on Cyprus dating from the Neolithic age, listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1998. It is known as one of the most important and best preserved prehistoric sites of the eastern Mediterranean. The site provides evidence of an organised functional society in the form of a collective settlement with surrounding fortifications for communal protection. It represents the Neolithic aceramic period, one of around 20 similar settlements spread throughout the island.

# History

The site was discovered in 1934 by Porphyrios Dikaios, director of the Department of Antiquities, who conducted six excavations between 1934 and 1946. Initially believed to date from around 4000 BC, radiocarbon dating revealed a foundation date of around 7000 BC, with occupation continuing until the 4th millennium BC. Further excavations were held in the early 1970s but were interrupted by the 1974 Turkish invasion of Cyprus. A French mission under Alain Le Brun resumed excavation in 1977. The village was suddenly abandoned around 6000 BC for unknown reasons, and the island appears to have remained uninhabited for about 1,500 years.

# What to see

The settlement features round stone structures called tholoi, between 2 and 9 meters in diameter, huddled close together within a protective stone wall 2.5 meters thick. These buildings are organized in tight groups around unroofed courtyards, believed to represent family groups. Each hut was divided internally with low walls, platforms, hearths, benches and windows, with evidence of piers to support upper floors in many cases. The dead were buried in crouched positions under the floors of the houses, with some provision made for offerings possibly indicating an ancestor cult. The population at any one time is thought not to have exceeded 300 to 600 inhabitants.

# Location & access

Khirokitia is situated on the slope of a hill in the valley of the Maroni River, towards the southern coast of Cyprus about 6 kilometers from the sea. The settlement occupied a strategic position cut off from the outside world except by the river. The site lies in the interior of the island's southern region.
